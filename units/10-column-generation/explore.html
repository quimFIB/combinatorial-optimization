The loop, iteration by iteration

Try: step and watch the dashed new pattern enter, the duals change, and the two bounds meet. Static version: the loop, on a small instance.

What it shows. The deck's cutting-stock instance: rolls of 10, pieces 3, 4 and 6 with demands 5, 3 and 2. Each state is one iteration: the restricted master's patterns (drawn as rolls cut into pieces) with their LP values $x_p$, the duals, the pattern the pricing knapsack returns (dashed, entering), its reduced cost, and the Farley bound. The chart tracks the master value falling and the best lower bound rising until they meet at 4.

Data. Recorded by slides/viz/traces/u10.py with unit 10's reference initial_patterns, solve_master, price and farley_bound; the numbers match the slide's table.

Smoothing the duals

Try: switch α and drag the iteration slider; compare how long the gap stays open. Static version: dual oscillation, and smoothing.

What it shows. A 25-width instance run three times with Wentges smoothing weight $\alpha = 0$ (no smoothing), 0.5 and 0.8. For each run: the master LP value and the best Farley bound per iteration. Early on the master value falls slowly while the bound lags far below; smoothing lifts the bound sooner and ends in fewer iterations (58 against 64 here).

Data. Recorded with unit 10's reference stabilised_column_generation on CuttingStock.random(25, seed=25, W=10000, low=0.01, high=0.2, demand=(10, 100)), the family the lab's then uses for smoothing.
