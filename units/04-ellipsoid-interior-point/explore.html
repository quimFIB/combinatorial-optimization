The ellipsoid method, one cut at a time

Try: step the deck's example, then the thin corner; on the empty set, tick "zoom to the ellipse" and watch the area shrink. Static version: two ellipsoid steps.

What it shows. Each step: the current ellipse (solid), the previous one (dashed), the green target region, the centre, and the cut through the centre parallel to the most violated row, with the kept half shaded. The run replays the loop of the reference ellipsoid_feasible, calling the reference ellipsoid_step; centres and matrices are recorded by slides/viz/traces/u04.py, and the ellipses are drawn from them in the page.

  • The deck's example ($x + y \ge 3$): two cuts, as on the slide, centre $(1.96, 1.96)$.
  • A thin corner: six cuts; the ellipse flattens along the target's direction.
  • An empty set ($y \ge 0.2$ and $y \le 0.1$): every step keeps 0.77 of the area, forever. After enough steps no ball of the promised radius fits, and the method answers "empty".

Along the central path

Try: slide t from left to right; watch y approach (0, 0, 1.5, 0.5) and every y·s stay equal to 1/t. Static versions: the central path, duality on the path.

What it shows. The central path of $\max 2x + y$ on unit 01's polygon, from the reference central_path with $t_0 = 0.01$ and $\mu = 1.25$ (56 points, up to $t \approx 2100$). For each point: the slacks, the dual point $y = 1/(ts)$, the check $A^\top y = c$, and the gap $b^\top y - c^\top x$ against $m/t$.

  • At small $t$ the point sits near the analytic centre; as $t$ grows it bends toward $(2.5, 1.5)$.
  • Every product $y_i s_i$ equals $1/t$: approximate complementary slackness, the same value in every row.
  • The gap and $m/t$ agree to the centering tolerance (they differ in the fourth decimal at the largest $t$).

The barrier on one variable

Try: slide t over four orders of magnitude; the minimizer walks from ½ toward 1 and the gap is always 2/t. Static version: the barrier on one variable.

What it shows. $f_t(x) = -tx - \log x - \log(1 - x)$ for $\max x$ on $[0, 1]$, drawn in the page from the formula, with its minimizer from the closed form $x^*(t) = (t - 2 + \sqrt{t^2 + 4})/2t$, the dual point $y = (1/(tx), 1/(t(1-x)))$ and the gap. The walls at 0 and 1 are where the curve shoots up.
