Sinkhorn's plan, by temperature

Try: slide ε down to 0.1 and up to 5; drop the iterations to 1 and 2. Static version: Sinkhorn on a 2×2 assignment.

What it shows. The plan $P$ for costs $\begin{pmatrix}1 & 3\\ 2 & 5\end{pmatrix}$ from the reference sinkhorn, for ten temperatures and 1 to 30 iterations each, recorded by slides/viz/traces/u31.py together with round_to_permutation. The dashed line is the exact 2×2 limit, $a/(1 - a) = e^{-1/(2\varepsilon)}$.

  • At $\varepsilon = 1$ and 30 iterations: $0.378 / 0.622$, the slide's converged plan.
  • $\varepsilon = 0.5$: 0.269 / 0.731, "leans harder".
  • Small $\varepsilon$: close to the permutation, but the iterations converge more slowly. Large $\varepsilon$: close to ½ everywhere, fast.

Gumbel-max samples, piling up

Try: drag the slider from 1 sample to 20 000 and watch the bars settle onto the dashed softmax. Static version: Gumbel-max on three logits.

What it shows. Logits $(1, 0, -1)$; each sample is the reference gumbel_max_sample with numpy's default_rng(0), as on the slide, recorded as running counts at log-spaced sample sizes. At 20 000 draws the frequencies are 0.671, 0.242, 0.087, the slide's table. The hand-worked sample beside the slider uses default_rng(3), where the least likely option wins.
