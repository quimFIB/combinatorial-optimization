Pick a price, read the bound

Try: drag the dot to u = 1, 4/3 and 2; watch which items are bought and the sign of g. Static version: L(u) on a three-variable example.

What it shows. The deck's example, $\min 3x_1 + 5x_2 + 4x_3$ with the constraint $2x_1 + 4x_2 + 3x_3 \ge 5$ dualised. For each price $u$ the relaxation buys every item whose priced cost $c_j - u\,w_j$ is negative; $L(u)$ is $5u$ plus those costs. The curve, the table and the subgradient are computed in the page from the costs and weights recorded by slides/viz/traces/u11.py. The faint line is the affine piece of the current purchase: $L$ is the lowest of these pieces, which is why it is concave.

  • $u = 1$: nothing bought, $g = 5$: raise the price.
  • $u = 4/3$: the peak, $L = 19/3 \approx 6.33$, the LP bound; item 3 is exactly indifferent.
  • $u = 2$: everything bought, $g = -4$: lower it.

Subgradient steps

Try: step through λ fixed (it zig-zags forever), then switch to halving. Static version: subgradient steps, on the example.

What it shows. Twelve iterations of unit 11's reference subgradient_ascent on the example, with the Polyak step and target 7, the integer optimum. With $\lambda = 1$ fixed it bounces between $u = 1.4$ and $u = 1$ forever. With the reference's halving rule ($\lambda$ halved as soon as a step fails to set a new best, patience=1) it closes in on $u = 4/3$.

The halving run differs slightly from the slide's table after step 4: the slide halves $\lambda$ after taking the step, the reference halves it before computing the next one. Both climb toward 6.33.

1-trees, priced into a tour

Try: step the five cities (one step to a tour), then the seven, where the bound drops before it climbs. Static version: a 1-tree on five cities.

What it shows. The minimum 1-tree under the current vertex penalties $\pi$, its degrees, and the Lagrangian bound. Vertices with degree above 2 (red) get dearer, leaves (outlined) cheaper, and the next 1-tree changes shape. When every degree is 2 the 1-tree is a tour and the bound equals its length.

  • Five cities (the deck's matrix, drawn schematically): 13, then 14 = the optimal tour.
  • Seven random cities (TSP.random(7, seed=2), drawn at their coordinates): 218, then 197 (a step down), 242 and 246, down again to 223.5, then up to 266, the optimal tour.

Data. Unit 11's reference one_tree and held_karp_bound; the optimal tours by brute force.
