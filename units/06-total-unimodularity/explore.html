Determinants, one submatrix at a time

Try: on the triangle, pick all three rows and columns; then press "check every square submatrix" on each matrix. Static version: TU, checked by hand.

What it shows. A matrix is totally unimodular when every square submatrix has determinant $-1$, $0$ or $1$. Click row and column headers to pick one submatrix and see its determinant; the button enumerates all of them and reports the counts, selecting the first bad one if there is any. Everything is computed in the page from the matrices written in this file.

  • The shift matrix gives the deck's counts: 12, 18 and 4 submatrices, all determinants 0 or 1.
  • The triangle is fine until the full $3\times3$, determinant 2.
  • The even 4-cycle is TU, the odd 5-cycle is not: its full matrix has determinant 2, the odd-cycle obstruction of "The odd cycle".

06 · explore

Ghouila-Houri signings, by hand

Try: on the triangle, sign all three rows and search for a good signing; then "test every row subset". Static version: a signing, worked.

What it shows. Ghouila-Houri: a matrix is TU exactly when every subset of its rows can be signed $\pm$ so that every column of the signed sum is in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. Click a row's sign cell to include it with $+$ or $-$; the bottom row is the signed sum. The search button tries every signing of the chosen rows (the first sign can be fixed).

  • Shift matrix, all four rows: alternate $+, -, +, -$ and every column sum is 0 or 1.
  • Triangle, all three rows: every signing leaves some column at $\pm 2$. A good signing of a graph's vertex rows would be a 2-colouring, and a triangle has none.
  • 4-cycle: sign vertices $+, -, +, -$ around the cycle, the bipartite colouring.
