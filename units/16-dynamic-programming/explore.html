The knapsack table, one cell at a time

Try: step and watch each cell look at the cell above and at the one a weight to the left; the last step reads the answer back. Static version: the knapsack table, filled in.

What it shows. The slide's instance (capacity 6; items of weight 2, 3, 4, 5 and value 3, 4, 5, 9) filled row by row. Each cell is the better of two older cells: skip the item (the cell above) or take it (the row above, its weight further left, plus its value). The green cells are the ones the current cell reads. The last step walks back up from the bottom-right cell to recover which items were taken.

The run is recorded by slides/viz/traces/u16.py, which writes out the recurrence cell by cell; its answer, 9 with item 4 alone, is checked against the reference knapsack, which fills the same table one row at a time.

In and out, bottom up

Try: step up the tree, leaves first, then back down as the cover is read off. Static version: in and out, on a seven-vertex tree.

What it shows. The slide's weighted tree. Going up, each vertex gets two numbers: in, the cheapest cover of its subtree that uses it (its weight plus the better of each child's two numbers), and out, the cheapest that doesn't (every child's in). Going down, the root takes its better number, and each child is forced in when its parent is out.

The run follows the reference tree_vertex_cover (children before parents, then a read-back from the root) and is checked against its answer, the cover $\{1, 2\}$ of weight 6.
