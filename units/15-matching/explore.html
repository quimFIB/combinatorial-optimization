König's cover, built from a matching

Try: step from the free left vertex along alternating paths; the last step turns what was reached into a cover. Static version: König's cover, on a small graph.

What it shows. The slide's bipartite graph with the maximum matching the reference hopcroft_karp finds, $\{\ell_0 r_0, \ell_2 r_1\}$. The search starts at the free left vertex and follows alternating paths: an unmatched edge to the right, the matched edge back. The cover is the left vertices not reached plus the right vertices reached, and it has the matching's size, so each proves the other optimal.

A logged copy of the reference konig_cover, checked against it.

The Hungarian method, potential by potential

Try: step and watch zeros appear as the potentials shift; the assigned cells are always zeros. Static version: the Hungarian method, on a 3×3 matrix.

What it shows. The slide's cost matrix. Each cell shows its reduced cost $c_{ij} - u_i - v_j$ (large) and its cost (small). Rows are added one at a time; the search tree's rows and columns are highlighted, and each shift adds $\delta$ to the tree rows' $u$ and subtracts it from the tree columns' $v$, which keeps every reduced cost $\ge 0$ and creates a new zero. When a zero reaches a free column, the assignment grows along the tight path.

At the end the assigned cells are all zero and every other cell is $\ge 0$: the potentials are a dual certificate, and $\sum u + \sum v = 5$ = the cost. The run is a logged copy of the reference hungarian, checked against its assignment and potentials.

Gale–Shapley, proposal by proposal

Try: follow C down its list while X and Y trade up. Static version: Gale–Shapley, round by round.

What it shows. The slide's preferences. Each step is one proposal: accepted by a free receiver, accepted with the previous partner dropped, or rejected. Proposers only move down their lists (brackets mark choices used up) and receivers only trade up, which is why it ends, and why the result has no blocking pair.

A logged copy of the reference gale_shapley; its matching is checked against the reference and by is_stable.
