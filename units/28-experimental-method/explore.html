A performance profile, with solvers switched off

Try: switch Y off and watch X's curve jump at τ = 1; then switch X off instead. Static version: a performance profile, by hand.

What it shows. The slide's three solvers on four instances (times in seconds, "fail" for a timeout). For whichever solvers are switched on, the curves are the reference performance_profile: the share of instances each solver solves within a factor τ of the fastest solver present. The table shows each ratio and the fastest solver per instance, from the reference virtual_best. All seven subsets are recorded by slides/viz/traces/u28.py; highlighted cells differ from the three-solver table.

  • With all three: X and Y start at 0.5 (they tie on p3), only Y reaches 1.
  • Without Y: X becomes the fastest on p2, its ratio there drops from 2 to 1, and its curve starts at 0.75. X's times didn't change; its competition did. That is Gould and Scott's trap.
  • Without X: Y's ratio on p1 becomes $4/3$ (against Z's 3 s), a step that wasn't in any three-solver curve.

A bootstrap interval, resample by resample

Try: drag the slider from 5 resamples to 2 000 and watch the interval settle at [0.68, 0.98]. Static version: a bootstrap interval in numbers.

What it shows. The slide's five per-instance time ratios, 0.6, 0.8, 0.9, 1.1, 0.7. Each resample draws five of them with replacement and takes the mean; the histogram shows the first $n$ resample means and the shaded band the 95% percentile interval. The resamples are drawn exactly as the reference bootstrap_ci draws them (random.Random(1), rng.choices), and each interval is bootstrap_ci's own answer for that number of resamples, checked against the recorded means.

  • 5 resamples: [0.76, 0.84], the slide's five means. 50: [0.70, 0.92]. From about 200 on it stays at [0.68, 0.98], wobbling by one resample at the top.
  • The interval's width comes from the data (five instances), not from the resample count. To make it narrower, benchmark more instances.
  • The means can only take a few values here (sums of five of the ratios, divided by 5), so the histogram has gaps: a reminder of how little information five instances carry.
