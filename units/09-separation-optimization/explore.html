Stoer–Wagner, phase by phase

Try: step through the four-vertex graph, then the six cities. Static version: the oracle, on six cities.

What it shows. The global minimum cut by Stoer–Wagner. Each phase builds a maximum-adjacency order (filled: just added; ringed: added earlier; numbers: how strongly each remaining group is attached), takes the last group's attachment as a candidate cut (red edges), and merges the last two groups (shaded bands).

On the six cities the best cut is $\{3, 4, 5\}$ with weight 1: below 2, so the subtour constraint for that set is violated even though every degree is 2 and the support graph is connected. Recorded by slides/viz/traces/u09.py, which copies the reference min_cut loop with a record of every step and checks its result against the reference function.

Separating by optimizing over the polar

Try: drag y inside and outside the square; switch to the triangle and the hexagon. Static version: a square and its polar.

What it shows. For $P = \{x : a_i \cdot x \le 1\}$ the polar $P^\circ$ is the convex hull of the rows $a_i$. Deciding whether $y$ is in $P$, and finding a violated constraint if not, is maximizing $a \cdot y$ over $P^\circ$: the best vertex is the most violated constraint. On the right, the dashed line $\{a : a \cdot y = 1\}$ keeps all of $P^\circ$ on one side exactly when $y \in P$. Everything is computed in the page from the constraints written in this file.
