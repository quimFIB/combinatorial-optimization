A 2×2 matrix is two vectors

Try: slide the entry from −½ (120°) to −1 (opposite) and past it. Static version: PSD on 2×2 matrices.

What it shows. The matrix with 1 on the diagonal and $c$ off it. Its eigenvalues are $1 \pm c$, both nonnegative exactly when $|c| \le 1$; then it is the Gram matrix of two unit vectors at angle $\arccos c$. Computed in the page; no recorded data.

Goemans–Williamson, with a hyperplane you can turn

Try: turn the hyperplane on the 5-cycle (every position cuts 4), then on the random weighted graph, where positions differ. Static version: GW on the 5-cycle.

What it shows. The SDP vectors, one per vertex, drawn in their plane; a hyperplane through the centre (drag its normal); the two sides; and which edges it cuts (green) or not (red, dashed). On the right: the SDP bound, the exact expected cut, the optimum, and a histogram of the cut weights of 2000 recorded random hyperplanes, with the expected cut and the SDP bound marked.

  • Odd cycles: neighbours sit far apart, every hyperplane cuts all but one edge, and the rounding always finds the optimum. The loss is all between the SDP and the integers.
  • A random weighted graph on 8 vertices: its optimal vectors happen to lie in a plane too, and hyperplanes now give different cuts; the average stays above 0.878 of the SDP bound.

Data: slides/viz/traces/u25.py, from the reference maxcut_sdp, vectors_from_gram, expected_cut, hyperplane_round (seeded) and gw_constant; optima by brute force. The vectors are drawn in the plane of the Gram matrix's top two eigenvectors, which holds them exactly (rank 2).
